        • About us

          The goals of 【SmartAction】

          Helping people with dementia stay “smart”,

          and people with Parkinson’s disease stay active (“action”).

          A shared bond among doctors, social workers, patients and families that began at Kaohsiung Chang Gung Memorial Hospital,

          rooted in patient groups jointly organized by the hospital’s Social Services Department and neurologists.

          Watch on YouTube: Kaohsiung SmartAction Growth Association anthem — choir version

          Watch on YouTube: SmartAction social dance, student practice ROC 106.09.13 [Buying and Selling Love]

          The association’s missions are:

          1. Provide information on the varied needs of people living with neurodegenerative diseases and their families, and compile

          a social resources handbook.

          2. As a third-sector (nonprofit) organization, seek collaboration with the first sector (public

          sector) and second sector (market sector) to integrate resources for neurodegenerative diseases

          or establish strategic alliances.

          3. Advocate for rights related to healthcare, daily living, welfare, policy and legislation for neurodegenerative diseases

          and associated interests.

          4. Increase public understanding of neurodegenerative diseases.

          5. Use public education to change social attitudes and values so that people with neurodegenerative

          diseases and their families face fewer tangible and intangible barriers.

          6. Offer continuing education on neurodegenerative diseases in a variety of formats and related research.。

          7. Serve the association’s members through gatherings and the sharing of ideas and feelings.

          Association bylaws

          I am very happy to share with everyone my experience at

          the SmartAction Association welfare organization and what I have gained there,

          and to tell you that being ill is not frightening; what is frightening is

          being unable to bravely take that crucial first step forward out of self-isolation.

          Standing here, I am proof that I absolutely can do it.

          Let my experience serve as a small example: if you have a family member like me,

          help them break free of the limitations in their mind,
